<div class="bilingual-block">
  <p class="en-prose">
    Landing in Tokyo for the first time in early October, the sheer density of visual stimuli is exhilarating. Stepping into the courtyard of Meiji Jingu, the noise of Harajuku fades instantly behind dense cedar groves. In the pebble courtyard, a traditional Shinto wedding procession moves in silent unison. A priest in white robes leads the bridal party, while a tall vermilion silk parasol shields the bride in her white shiromuku gown.
  </p>
  <p class="zh-prose">
    十月初第一次踏上东京的土地，城市扑面而来的密集感让人神经格外警觉。穿过明治神宫粗壮的原木鸟居，原宿的喧嚣在参天杉树林里骤然归于静谧。内庭的碎石地上，一场传统的神前婚礼队伍正缓步前行。神职人员着白袍引路，朱红色的油纸大伞为新娘撑开一方沉静的空间，木屐碾压在碎石上的沙沙声清晰可闻。
  </p>
</div>

<div class="bilingual-block">
  <p class="en-prose">
    The following day brought steady autumn rain across the metropolis. Looking up through a clear vinyl umbrella near Shiba Park, the orange latticework of Tokyo Tower sliced into the grey overcast sky. The smartphone camera in hand, light and unobtrusive, responded intuitively to the glistening asphalt, catching neon reflections that pooled in the gutters before rushing traffic wiped them clean.
  </p>
  <p class="zh-prose">
    次日细雨绵延。站在芝公园附近仰头望去，隔着透明伞面，东京塔橙红色的钢骨直挺挺地刺入灰白的雨云。手机拿在手里极轻便，在雨中无需繁琐架设，抬手便定格了地面微泛亮光的沥青路，街角红绿灯的倒影在水洼里化开，瞬间又被驶过的出租车带起一片水雾。
  </p>
</div>
